The Citation Record, Edition 2026.Q4 (forthcoming)

An independent quarterly benchmark measuring how accurately legal AI systems cite the law.

What this measures

Legal AI systems produce citations. Some of those citations are to cases that do not exist. Others are to real cases with the wrong reporter or page, real cases that say something other than what the system claims, real cases that have been overruled, or real cases from a jurisdiction that does not bind the court in question.

These are six different failures with six different professional consequences, and the standard verification a lawyer performs — confirming the case exists — catches only the first. Every vendor claim we surveyed reports existence, or reports nothing measurable at all. The Citation Record scores all six separately, against a ground-truth corpus of public-domain primary law, owned by none of the systems under test, and publishes the false-positive rate alongside the catch rate.

How it works

Edition One measures three raw models through their APIs: Claude, GPT-5, and Gemini. The same test items are put to each under a frozen prompt protocol, and practising attorneys score what comes back. Citation existence and accuracy resolve mechanically against CourtListener. Misattributed holdings, overruled authority, and jurisdictional weight require attorney judgment and receive it.

No subscription is required to take part, and no participant accepts a vendor’s terms of use. The research and drafting products built on these models are not measured this edition; testing those waits on a seated panel and on counsel.

No AI system scores any item. The scoring rubric is published in full, and the methodology and prompt protocol are published before any results are. The test items are pre-registered rather than published: fixed and hashed before testing, disclosed once the edition ships, and rotated for the next. Publishing them in advance would make contamination of later editions certain rather than possible. Each edition is free, carries a permanent identifier, and states the measurement window it describes.

Disclosures

No vendor pays anything

No measured vendor has paid, or will pay, any amount in any form: no participation fees, sponsorship, licensing, consulting, equity, referral commissions, or complimentary access beyond publicly available free tiers. This is a permanent commitment, not a policy for one edition.

The publisher sells work on this subject, and intends to sell a service built on this benchmark

The publisher sells a book on this subject and intends to offer an attestation service built on this benchmark’s methodology. A benchmark that is trusted and widely cited raises the value of both. Citation Firewall, a verification tool also built by the publisher, is not among the systems this benchmark tests. These interests are disclosed rather than resolved: nothing in the structure of the benchmark prevents its findings from serving the publisher’s other work.

The ground-truth provider integrates with systems under test, and the benchmark pays for access

Free Law Project publishes an MCP server that connects CourtListener to AI assistants over an open standard. It reaches three systems this benchmark tests: Claude, announced 12 May 2026; ChatGPT, named as a supported client; and Google Cloud’s Gemini Enterprise for Legal, announced 25 August 2026, which ships a citation verification capability. The publisher has held a paid Tier 1 CourtListener membership, $10 a month at published rates, since 7 September 2026. This is disclosed rather than resolved: the corpus is public-domain primary law, one server serves every client on the same terms, and the alternatives — Shepard’s, KeyCite — are owned outright by systems under test.

No non-disclosure agreements

The publisher has signed no NDA with any measured vendor and will not. Where a conflict cannot be disclosed, the affected system is withdrawn from testing and the withdrawal is stated.

Vendors see results before publication, and cannot change them

Each measured vendor receives its results 21 days before publication. Responses are printed unedited. Corrections are adopted where warranted; disputes are left standing on the record.

Taking part

The panel is being assembled. Attorneys who do litigation work are welcome to write, as are law librarians willing to review the methodology before it is fixed. Scoring calls for judgment about holdings, subsequent history and jurisdictional weight, not for access to any vendor’s system. The commitment is roughly ninety minutes per quarter. Contributors are credited by name; none are paid.
